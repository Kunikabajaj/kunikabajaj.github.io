Understanding prompt

Design a Flatmate Finder App – a platform that connects people looking for roommates or rental rooms with compatible matches based on lifestyle, habits, and preferences. Your design should balance functionality with personality – after all, choosing a flatmate is almost like finding a best friend.

Research

User Research

Research methods: I utilized two primary data collection techniques

🔍 Desk research📞 Interviews

I went through multiple research papers, articles, blogs, and user reviews to understand how people approach shared living. This included their needs, frustrations, and how they make decisions when choosing a flatmate or a place to stay. The goal was to build a clear understanding of the existing landscape before moving further.

Key insights

20-35is the largest age group among renters in India, making up 32% of the tenant population
18-25is the second-largest segment, reflecting a strong Gen Z presence in the shared-living market
75%of shared-living conflicts stem from lifestyle mismatches like smoking, noise, guests, and cleanliness

I conducted three telephonic interviews with individuals who had experience living with roommates, particularly strangers. I also attempted to speak with people who had listed their flats online to find roommates, but none of the participants had used dedicated platforms for that purpose. These conversations provided deeper insight into real user pain points, from lifestyle clashes to trust concerns, and helped uncover emotional and practical gaps that existing platforms often overlook.

This data helped shape a persona focused on users actively searching for shared flats or roommates. While I aimed to include flat listers, none had used dedicated platforms, so the persona reflects the seeker’s journey and needs.

User persona

Meet Riya

Riya is a young professional living in Bangalore who currently shares a flat with strangers. After a few mismatched experiences, she’s looking for a flatmate who better fits her lifestyle and habits.

Age: 26

Location: Bangalore

Profession: Content Strategist at an ed-tech startup

Living Situation: Currently lives in a shared 2BHK with two flatmates she found through Zolo and Facebook Marketplace

Goals

  • To find a flatmate who matches her lifestyle and values
  • To feel safe and comfortable sharing a home with someone new
  • To avoid repeating past mismatches due to habits or unclear expectations

Pain Points

  • Awkward conversations around cleaning and kitchen rules
  • Roommates who party late or bring guests over unexpectedly
  • Lack of clarity on roommate preferences before moving in
  • No way to filter listings by dealbreakers like food habits or noise levels
  • Trust issues with platforms – fake profiles or no reviews

Needs

  • To see lifestyle compatibility clearly (e.g. food, sleep, cleaning)
  • A gentle, privacy-respecting way to connect before sharing contact info
  • Verified profiles and listings to feel secure
  • A friendlier, Gen Z-style experience – less transactional, more human

Behavior

  • Browses rooms casually at first but takes her time before messaging
  • Prefers reading reviews and personal descriptions over just rent info
  • Wants to vibe-check potential flatmates – not just match rent and location
  • Would be more comfortable if the app felt conversational and empathetic

Ideate

Brainstorming

I explored around 20 to 30 ideas aimed at improving the overall user experience. Some focused on ensuring safety, simplifying steps, and reducing future conflicts or mismatched expectations. For example, letting users clearly state house rules while listing their flat, suggesting a flatmate checklist within chats to align early on, and adding reviews of both properties and flatmates to build trust. A few were more forward-looking, like enabling rent payment through the app or auto-saving contact names when reaching out to multiple listings, all to make the process smoother and smarter.

Wireframing

To evaluate what would work best for the two primary user types – those looking for a room or looking for a flatmate – I created two rough wireframes in my sketchbook. Create

Colors & Font

Orange

Orange radiates warmth and energy – it feels welcoming, youthful, and full of movement – making it perfect for a rental app that wants to feel human, modern, and ready for action.

Blue

Blue represents the more structured side of the app, especially for listings. It adds a sense of trust and stability while creating strong visual contrast against the brightness of yellow.
